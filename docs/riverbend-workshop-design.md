# Riverbend Workshop Design Foundation

## Finished Hearth Reference Experience

The hearth establishes soft illustrated realism and an evidence-led imagined past, rather than literal magic. Start in play with "Who made a home here?" and one visible pot edge. Each piece takes one Reveal and one Collect; restoring it exposes a continuous soot line and repair. Place the actual pot, then Imagine the room to crossfade into a modest cooking scene. Everyday cooking is best supported; Keeping supplies and Making things receive brief evidence-based feedback and never block progression. The doorway carries the question onward: this room later changed.

Use locally bundled Fraunces for 24px desktop / 20px mobile prompts, with moderate softness (SOFT 50), normal letterforms (WONK 0), and optical sizing. Alegreya Sans uses regular body text, medium helper text, and bold 16px action labels. Bundle each font's OFL license. Charcoal surfaces, ivory lettering, reed-green primary actions, and mineral-blue focus rings establish the shared palette. Objects and targets share a fitted scene frame so positions stay attached to the artwork. The cooking motif's "warm meal" label is a working interpretation, not a language translation. An optional saved hearth acknowledgement supplements existing saves without renaming keys.

Keep the same panel treatment and action hierarchy in all three memories. Join pieces / Bind pattern name the result even while disabled; one top prompt explains the current task. Hide collected clue labels once the object or pattern has been assembled or placed. Story choices expose their selected state with aria-pressed. Notebook stays inside the viewport and both Notebook and Menu close with Escape, returning focus to their disclosure. Doorway and bench memory captions sit in the screen layout rather than the cropped scene frame. All three memories use Imagine actions to indicate reconstruction from clues.

Riverbend Workshop is a compact archaeology-themed cozy mystery puzzle set in the fictional Leth River Basin. It focuses on the fantasy of waking a room through physical traces: reveal clues, collect pieces, restore an object, place it back into the room, and choose what the memory seems to mean.

The main game surface should feel like play first and archaeological reasoning second. Professional procedure, evidence metadata, confidence math, and detailed notes belong in the optional Notebook or in hidden support logic, not in the first playable loop.

Riverbend may gradually reveal a tiny visual mark vocabulary, but it should begin as recognition through objects rather than a language lesson. The first chapter teaches one mark: a warm meal sign that appears across the restored cooking pot and is recorded in the Notebook after the room memory wakes. The player now restores that pot through a forgiving hands-on fragment puzzle before carrying it back to the hearth. The next beat follows the doorway bead and teaches a kept-close mark. A third beat follows the bench, where small weights and a color clue become a work rhythm mark. Together, the three memories prove the core route without opening a full evidence dashboard.

## Site History

The structure is a mudbrick-and-timber room attached to a courtyard. Phase I used the room for domestic cooking and storage around a hearth. Phase II reduced hearth use and added a textile workbench, household repair, and small-scale pigment or dye finishing connected to river trade. Phase III briefly reused the room for storage, threshold repair, and possible planned closure before roof collapse and later disturbance complicated the record.

## Deposit Sequence

1. A packed clay floor, hearth, threshold, and north storage zone are built from local materials.
2. Cooking, sweeping, and foot traffic create hearth ash, cut bone, seed residue, and threshold wear.
3. A cooking pot is repaired, reused, and broken near the hearth.
4. A bench and textile work area replace the main domestic emphasis.
5. Spindle weights, fiber traces, seating wear, and imported pigment material accumulate around the bench.
6. The threshold is repaired with a reused stone block and ceramic packing.
7. Storage vessels and a bent fastener remain near the doorway during the final use.
8. A bead is placed or lost under the threshold edge, remaining intentionally ambiguous.
9. Roof collapse crushes storage material and scatters peg fragments.
10. A later pit cuts through earlier surfaces and moves several diagnostic fragments.

## Evidence Logic

Physical joins carry the highest confidence: cooking pot fragments 1-3, storage jar fragments 7-8, pigment cup fragments 13-14, and roof peg fragments 21-22. Functional groups remain interpretive: cooking, textile work, repair, storage, closure, trade, collapse, and disturbance. A player can support a reading without proving every component, and later finds can complicate earlier choices.

## Major Interpretations

- Sudden abandonment after damage: supported by roof collapse, crushed storage, objects left in place, and the bent fastener; weakened by threshold repair, bead placement, and pit mixing.
- Planned closure of a reused workshop: supported by threshold repair, bead placement, textile evidence, and storage near the doorway; complicated by collapse evidence and displaced fragments.
- Later disturbance created a false pattern: supported by the pit cut, intrusive glazed sherd, displaced pigment cup base, and moved pot base; weakened by coherent evidence outside the disturbed area.

The intended strongest reading is planned closure of a reused workshop, while the bead remains plausible rather than confirmed.

## Replay Constraints

### Closing The Room Story

After the bench decision, `See the room's story` brings the three discoveries together in a short, choice-sensitive ending. Meals precede textile work; the doorway hints at later change. The bead's owner and purpose remain unresolved. This is an evidence-led reading, not a translated language or certain account of a person's intentions. The closing view is saved, and players can return to revise their choice without losing discoveries. Learned memories can be revisited from the optional Notebook. The hearth includes a `Then` / `Now` comparison between the imagined reconstruction and the excavated scene; it does not change evidence or progress.

Fieldwork Mode may vary positions, preservation quality, tool pressure, and whether ambiguous finds appear. It must use the same authored evidence families and derive deposits from coherent site events rather than random scattering.

## Interaction Requirements

The player must be able to complete the main route without typing or opening optional notes. The first loop uses five visible verbs: Reveal, Collect, Restore, Place, and Decide. Restore is a small tactile puzzle: move and rotate two fragments until a soft preview appears, then join them. Place is also playable: carry the restored pot, bead, or bench pattern to the glowing room target, then set it down. The second memory reuses the same verbs with fewer steps: Reveal the bead, Collect it, Place it by the doorway, wake the doorway memory, and Decide what it suggests. The third memory adds a compact bench pattern puzzle: collect two small weights and a color clue, move them into a simple rhythm, place the pattern at the bench, wake the bench memory, and Decide what the bench remembered. Desktop and mobile should keep one large playable scene, one primary action, and at most two active choices before the final Decide moment, with no browser-page scrolling during active play.
